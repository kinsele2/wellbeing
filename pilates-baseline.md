**Last updated**: August 2026 (Week 8 of 8-week re-entry plan)
**Status**: Active — **Tuesday 7:30am** 30-minute anchor session, moved from Saturday in Week 2 of the re-entry plan and held every week since. The move is permanent and has been the single most successful structural change in the exercise side of the project.

**Scope note**: this file covers the Tuesday anchor session only. The two mid-week sessions are specified in **`pilates-wfh-session.md`**, which is authoritative for their content and timing.

## Goals This Practice Serves

1. **Running support** — maintaining 15k/week (1 x 7km, 1 x 8km treadmill)
2. **Golf** — 18 holes most weeks, sometimes 27–36 in summer; thoracic rotation is the primary physical demand
3. **Vitality and agility** — general feeling of strength and freedom of movement day-to-day
4. **Lower back strength and resilience** — overlaps with all three goals above

## Exercise Value Assessment (Against Goals)

### High Value — Retain

| Exercise | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Bird Dog | Core stability, lower back, glute activation — directly supports running and lower back resilience |
| Bridge + Bridge Single Leg Circles | Glute strength and hip stability — core running and lower back exercise |
| Bridge Figure Four Hip Dips ⚠️ | Piriformis and external hip rotator opening — critical for both running and golf; hip dip adds glute med work |
| Reverse Table Top Leg Lifts ⚠️ | Posterior chain — glutes, hamstrings, lower back working in extension; high value for running resilience |
| Swan / Belly Swimming | Posterior chain extension, lower back strength |
| Forearm Plank | Core stability |
| Side Lying series | Hip abductor strength — knee tracking and running stability |
| Supine Windshield Wiper | Lumbar rotation mobility — directly addresses lower back stiffness |
| Supine Pelvic Lifts | Glute activation, lower back |

### Moderate Value — Keep but Lower Priority

| Exercise | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Bridge Leg Lifted Heel Raises ⚠️ | Calf and ankle stability — real but narrow payoff relative to difficulty; not a priority for running or golf specifically |
| Double Leg Stretch | Core flexion work — useful but not targeted to primary goals |
| Supine Leg Circles | Hip mobility — useful but lower priority than rotation work |
| Kneeling Leg Lifts | Hip flexor and glute — moderate overlap with higher-value exercises |

### Lower Priority — Candidates for Swapping Out

| Exercise | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Hundred | Classical Pilates — primarily flexion-based; limited contribution to running, golf, or lower back goals |
| Rolling Like a Ball | Classical Pilates — spinal massage effect; low functional contribution to specific goals |

## Identified Gap — Thoracic Rotation

This session is strong on hip and posterior chain work but **notably light on thoracic (mid-back) rotation**. This is the single most important physical component for golf, and it also protects the lower back by ensuring rotation happens through the thoracic spine rather than the lumbar spine.

**Status**: this gap is now addressed by the two mid-week sessions in `pilates-wfh-session.md`, where Thread the Needle is the priority exercise in both. It is deliberately *not* solved by lengthening or altering this Tuesday session — the problem was never volume, it was frequency. One dose per week partially resets before the next one, so each session becomes re-establishment rather than consolidation.

If a change to this session is ever wanted, Rolling Like a Ball or the Hundred are the first candidates for replacement by Thread the Needle. But this is optional — the mid-week sessions are the actual fix.

## Morning Mobility Sequence — DORMANT

**Status as of August 2026**: designed but never started, and never once recorded in the weekly log across eight weeks. Marked dormant rather than deleted.

**Why it's dormant**: the symptoms it was designed for — lower back ache on standing, general morning stiffness — have substantially reduced through the Tuesday session and the golf pre-swing primer. The daily-habit format also carries the highest decision-load cost of anything in the project, which is precisely why it never started. Adding it now would compete with the two mid-week sessions for the same limited attention budget.

**Revisit if**: morning stiffness or standing-up ache returns and persists for more than two or three weeks despite the Tuesday session and mid-week work running normally.

The sequence, preserved for that eventuality:

| Exercise | Duration | Purpose |
|----------|----------|---------|
| Cat-Cow | 60 seconds | Wakes up entire spine, lumbar to thoracic; gentlest entry point |
| Child's Pose with lateral reach | 30 sec each side | Thoracic stretch |
| Supine Windshield Wiper | 60 seconds | Lumbar rotation stiffness; directly addresses standing-up ache |
| Supine Figure Four Stretch | 60 sec each side | Piriformis and external hip rotator release |
| Bridge Hold | 30–45 seconds | Glute and lower back activation before first stand of the day — priming, not training |

**Total**: 5–6 minutes, floor-based, before first coffee. Consistency over intensity; if a morning is missed, don't compensate.

| Weeks 1–2 | Aches may worsen temporarily; BodyPump soreness normal | Confirmed — soreness Tue–Fri after Week 1 BodyPump, as expected |
| Weeks 3–4 | Standing-up ache and general stiffness noticeably reduce | Confirmed, though Weeks 3–4 also produced the predicted fatigue trough. Week 4 resolved within 24–48 hours via one good night's sleep plus the Monday run |
| Weeks 6–8 | Recognisably like pre-holiday baseline | Confirmed and arguably exceeded — Week 6 reported inner stability and reserve capacity in running described as unlike previous attempts; Week 7 produced the strongest Pilates and BodyPump sessions of the block |
| Months 3–4 | Genuinely strong and agile; noticed in retrospect | Still ahead. This is the horizon the mid-week sessions are aimed at |

**Important**: diet and exercise interact directly on this timeline. Inflammation, blood sugar instability and alcohol all slow recovery. Improving both simultaneously is what produces meaningful change — the Week 7 cheat day is a small live demonstration, where a single poor-diet day produced a physical reaction that would have gone unnoticed eight weeks earlier.

## Key Principles for This Practice

- **The Tuesday 7:30am 30-minute session is the anchor.** Frequency increases are staged additions, never simultaneous commitments.
- **Pilates is historically the first session to drop** when the routine breaks down. It is therefore the leading indicator of plan integrity — if Pilates slips, the rest is already in trouble. Protecting it during breaks matters more than protecting BodyPump.
- **Difficulty ≠ benefit**: the Heel Raises are hard but moderate value; the Figure Four Hip Dips and Reverse Table Top Leg Lifts are hard and high value. The distinction matters when deciding what to keep on a low-motivation week.
- **Thoracic rotation is the identified gap**, and it is a frequency problem, not a volume problem. See `pilates-wfh-session.md`.
- **Anchoring beats willpower.** Moving this session from Saturday to Tuesday morning — placing it on a day already committed to exercise — converted it from the most-dropped session to one described as an unmissable part of the week. That mechanism, not increased motivation, is what fixed it.
