# PART 1 — THE PHYSICAL PROTOCOL

## The Problem

- Practice swings happen mid-workday: up from desk, downstairs, straight into full-speed swings in the back garden, no ball, no transition.
- Thoracic spine (the identified mobility gap) is stiffest right after sitting. When it can't supply the rotation, the lumbar spine compensates — the same pattern causing post-round soreness, but loaded daily instead of weekly.
- Sitting also tightens hip flexors, restricting hip rotation and pushing more compensatory load onto the lumbar spine from both ends of the chain.
- Result: soreness that used to run Sunday-through-Friday from the round alone was being reinforced daily by unprimed full-speed swings.
- This is a preparation problem, not an age limit — fixable with sequencing changes, not a reason to stop practising.

---

## The Pre-Swing Primer (60–90 seconds)

Done every time, immediately before practice swings — attached to the existing desk-to-garden transition, not treated as a separate task.

1. **5–6 slow standing thoracic rotations** — arms crossed over chest, rotate shoulders while keeping hips square ("open book" standing version)
2. **5–6 hip hinges or bodyweight squats** — wakes up the hips after sitting
3. **First 3–4 swings at half speed**, ramping to full speed by swing 5–6

Purpose: stops daily re-aggravation of the compensation pattern. This is damage control, not capacity building — it prevents things getting worse but doesn't rebuild capacity on its own.

**Extended version before a round**: several minutes of the same thoracic rotations and hip work, then a handful of half-speed-to-full swings. Never start a round cold from the car. See the state layer in Part 2 — the primer is performance equipment as well as back protection.

---

## Thread the Needle — Correct Form

- On all fours (tabletop position)
- One arm threads under the body, rotating through the **mid-back** (thoracic spine)
- **Hips stay square to the floor throughout** — the most common failure point. If the hips rotate along with the shoulders, the movement is happening in the lumbar spine instead, which defeats the purpose
- Movement should feel like it's happening between the shoulder blades, not the lower back
- Go slower than feels necessary — deliberately controlled, not a stretch to reach further

# PART 2 — THE PERFORMANCE PROTOCOL

## The Core Diagnosis

The deliberate, conscious-control mode that makes you excellent at engineering **actively degrades golf performance**, where automatic execution and external focus are what allow a good swing to happen.

Constant mid-week mechanical tinkering and swing-thought switching get carried onto the course. The practical effect is that each round is played on a *freshly rebuilt swing* rather than a stable motor program. That, not talent variance, is the mechanism behind a superb round and a 106 within days of each other.

**The strategic consequence**: the path to sub-10 is almost entirely **floor-raising, not ceiling-raising**. The ceiling is already sub-10 golf. The handicap is hostage to the floor. That's where the strokes are.

---

## 1. The Practice/Play Wall

On the course, the swing is finished goods. You do not open the wiring while trying to use it.

Mechanical work happens in practice. Play is execution only.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This wall is the single most important rule in this file.

## 2. How to Practise

- Pick **one** mechanical focus, chosen against **objective feedback** (pro, video, launch monitor) — never against feel, which shifts day to day.
- Commit to it for a **defined block of minimum 3–4 weeks**, and do not change it mid-block regardless of results.
- Expect it to get **worse before it stabilises**. Every instinct will say abandon it after one bad session. That urge is the exact thing that's been causing the damage. A swing thought only becomes automatic if it survives long enough to sink below conscious control — weeks, not days.

## 3. How to Play

Pre-shot routine, identical every time:

1. Behind the ball, pick a **small specific target** — a branch, a discoloured patch. Not "the fairway."
2. One rehearsal swing for feel, one breath.
3. Step in, last look at the **target**, swing with one constant: **tempo**.

At most **one** swing thought over the ball, always a rhythm or tempo feel ("smooth", a count) — **never a body-part instruction**. Same routine, same pace, every shot, including the good ones and especially after a bad one.

A bad shot is accepted flatly, then the identical routine runs on the next ball. That acceptance is not decoration — it is the circuit-breaker that stops the "one bad drive → start fixing → degrade the next → panic" spiral that produced the 106.

## 4. The State Layer

Three variables quietly move the millisecond timing that determines whether the kinetic chain sequences properly:

- **Warm-up** — never start a round cold. Run the primer's thoracic rotations and hip work, then a handful of half-to-full swings.
- **Sleep** — broken sleep degrades fine-motor timing. A round after a bad night starts with a hidden handicap you can't feel.
- **Alcohol the night before** — the same next-day mechanism already documented for Friday concentration, arriving at the first tee. Keep the count low before a round. This slots directly into the existing alcohol-reduction lever.

# PART 3 — THE PORTUGAL BLOCK

**8 weeks, 21 July → 17 September 2026.**

Deliberately weighted **away from mechanics**. Eight weeks grooves one change, not a swing overhaul — and the deadline plus the embarrassment of the 106 are exactly the pressure that triggers the quick-fix/switch failure mode.

The biggest bankable gain before September is **not falling apart**: converting a disaster round from 106 into roughly 90 by process alone. That's 15+ strokes, requires zero mechanical change, and directly targets the thing that caused the frustration.

## Weeks 1–4 (21 Jul – 17 Aug) — One mechanical block + process install

- **Mechanical focus: setup fundamentals only** — grip, alignment, ball position, posture. Highest-return, lowest-risk, most repeatable elements in golf, and the most systemizable: static, checkable, objective. Inconsistent setup is also a hidden answer to "I was doing the same thing" — a couple of degrees of alignment or ball-position drift makes an identical swing produce different outcomes. **Verify against video or a pro, not feel.**
- **Process**: install the pre-shot routine on every range session and every round from week 1. Practice/Play wall active immediately.

## Weeks 5–8 (18 Aug – 15 Sep) — Hold and consolidate

- **No new mechanical change.** Groove the setup change until it's automatic.
- Deepen process discipline: full flat acceptance of bad shots, zero mid-round fixing. Explicit target: convert one disaster round into a ~90 by process alone.
- Rehearse trip conditions where possible — multiple rounds close together, unfamiliar course.

## Weekly video review

- Send one clip from a practice session each week.
- The question each week is deliberately narrow: **is the one focus visibly improving — hold or progress?** Not "what else is wrong."
- **Role split**: a PGA pro with a launch monitor for objective mechanical diagnosis; the weekly review here as the **between-lessons process guardian** — a second pair of eyes on the gross stuff (setup, posture, alignment, ball position, tempo, over-the-top, sequencing, ball flight if filmed) and, more importantly, the enforcer of one-change-at-a-time.
- **Not reliably measurable from phone video**: precise clubface angle and club path — both are angle-dependent.
- **Strongly recommended**: a few PGA-pro launch-monitor sessions before Portugal, to give the objective read video can't fully provide and to choose the setup change against ball flight rather than feel.

## Key Principles

- **Do your changing in practice, do your trusting in play, and pick one thing and leave it alone long enough to become automatic.** This is the whole file in one sentence.
- Primer prevents daily re-aggravation; Pilates rebuilds underlying capacity. Neither substitutes for the other — both are needed.
- Frequency matters more than volume for thoracic adaptation. This is why a single weekly 30-second dose wasn't moving the needle, and why the mid-week sessions matter more than lengthening the Tuesday one.
- **Post-round recovery *duration* is the tracking signal, not the presence of soreness.** Some soreness after 18 holes is normal. A shortening window is the adaptation signal.
- This protocol was prompted by recognising that daily practice swings, not just the weekly round, were driving the back pain — worth remembering as a reference case for spotting similar patterns elsewhere in the routine.
- The floor is where the strokes are. Not the ceiling.
