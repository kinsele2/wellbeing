## Notes on Each Session

**Treadmill Running (Mon 8km / Thu 7km)**
Switched from outdoor running this year. The switch was deliberate and has been beneficial — significantly improved recovery time. Outdoor running was causing joint stress; treadmill has addressed this. This is a long-term sustainable change.

**BodyPump**
Resistance-based group class. High value for maintaining muscle and overall conditioning. Rarely missed — partly because the consequences of a gap are immediately felt (significant muscle soreness on return), which acts as a deterrent to skipping.

**Pilates (Saturday)**
30-minute session. First to be dropped when routine breaks down — flagged as "difficult to push myself to do, as it is difficult." However, acknowledged as hugely beneficial, particularly for the core mobility and feeling-of-youthfulness goals that are central to this project. Protecting Pilates should be a priority even when the wider routine slips, as it is likely the highest-value session for long-term joint health and mobility.

---

## Breakdown Pattern

When the routine deteriorates, the typical sequence is:

1. **Pilates drops first** (Saturday session — perceived effort too high relative to motivation)
2. **One of the runs drops** (usually the Thursday 7km)
3. **BodyPump is retained longest** (pain deterrent keeps attendance up)

Understanding this pattern is useful for rebuilding: the goal when returning from a break is not to immediately restore the full routine, but to hold BodyPump as the anchor and add back the other sessions progressively.

> [!NOTE]
> Status as of August 2026: step 1 of this pattern has been structurally addressed. Moving Pilates to Tuesday morning removed the standalone-session problem that made it the first casualty. The pattern should still be treated as the live risk model during breaks and disrupted weeks — Pilates remains the leading indicator of plan integrity — but the specific Saturday failure mode no longer exists.

## Current Status — August 2026, Week 8 of the re-entry plan

**The active routine:**

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Monday | 8km treadmill run |
| Tuesday | Pilates 7:30am (30 min) + BodyPump 7pm |
| Thursday | 7km treadmill run |

Held at 4/4 for seven consecutive weeks. This is longer than the April block, which ran 4–5 weeks before breaking down.

**What changed versus April, and why it matters:**

- **Pilates moved from Saturday to Tuesday 7:30am** in Week 2, and has not been missed since. This is the single most significant structural change in the project. Placing it on a day already committed to exercise converted it from the most-dropped session into one described as an unmissable part of the week. The breakdown pattern below was solved by relocation, not by motivation.
- **Both runs held**, including through the Weeks 3–4 fatigue trough and the Week 5 motivation dip. The Thursday run — historically the second thing to drop — has held every week, and floats to Friday when needed rather than being lost.
- **The bulk of the week is complete by 8pm Tuesday**, which has itself become a protective feature.

**Two mid-week Pilates sessions** (mobility and stability) are designed and not yet started — see `pilates-wfh-session.md`. First exposure planned during the Weeks 9–10 break, full start Week 11.

**Weeks 9–10 are annual leave.** See `holiday-deload-plan-weeks9-10.md` — the first deliberately designed reduced-volume break rather than a full stop.

> [!NOTE]
> Historical note: the period from roughly the start of April onward saw a gradual breakdown of the routine, coinciding with the return of poor dietary habits. The two were linked, and that linkage remains the project's core assumption.

## Historical Context

- Regular exercise since late teens / early twenties
- Previously ran outdoors for many years; switched to treadmill this year for joint health
- Exercise has historically partially offset poor diet, but this is becoming less true with age
- The tipping point feeling this year — that exercise is becoming "genuinely more difficult" — is likely multi-factorial: age, diet quality, alcohol intake, and possible cumulative joint load from years of outdoor running

## Key Observations for Future Reference

- Treadmill switch was the right call — do not revert to outdoor running
- BodyPump absence causes pain on return; this is a useful built-in deterrent to missing it but also means post-holiday re-entry will be uncomfortable regardless
- Pilates is high value but low motivation — worth noting when it is completed as a positive marker
- Energy levels and exercise motivation appear closely correlated with diet quality in the same or preceding week
