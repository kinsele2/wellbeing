---

## Why This File Exists

Every working part of this project is a behaviour attached to a pre-existing anchor: Pilates to Tuesday 7:30am, the primer to the desk-to-garden transition, porridge to getting up, Click & Collect to a fixed day. Holidays delete all of those anchors in a single step, which leaves intention with nothing to attach to.

The result is a documented pattern: genuinely wanting to do things — the game, family time, house jobs — and being unable to initiate any of them. This is an initiation problem, not a motivation problem. The motivation is present and intact throughout.

This file supplies one artificial anchor per day to replace the ones the break removed.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The purpose is not productivity. It is giving the day a shape so the rest of it becomes usable. A day with one completed anchor and eight hours of gaming is a success. A day with no anchor and eight hours of sitting is the pattern this exists to break.

---

## The Rules

Decided in advance, deliberately, so they don't have to be negotiated in the moment.

1. **One activity per day.** Not a list. A list is unstartable.
2. **Attached to a fixed point in the day** — after breakfast is the default. The time is decided once, not daily.
3. **It must have a defined first physical action.** "Clean the office" is unstartable. "Empty the cupboard" is startable. If the first action isn't obvious, the task isn't specified yet.
4. **It must have a visible end state.** A shelf, a drawer, a surface, a stage. Something you can look at and know it's finished.
5. **Twenty minutes is the default size.** Larger jobs are allowed, but must be broken into stages first (see below).
6. **Decide what counts as the win before starting**, not afterwards.
7. **Everything after the anchor is free, and guiltlessly so.** That is the point of doing it.

> [!NOTE]
> The anchor does not have to be a chore. Initiation is failing uniformly across everything, including the things actively wanted. Some days the anchor should be the game, or something with the family. Fixing initiation only for worthy tasks fixes the wrong half of the problem.

---

## Stages, Not Projects

Large jobs get broken into an ordered sequence before starting. Each stage completed is a real result on its own.

The failure mode to design against is the **point of no return** — a stage after which stopping leaves things worse than before starting (e.g. emptying a room). Identify it in advance and decide whether today has the capacity to get past it.

> [!WARNING]
> Low periods have a documented tendency to revise positive evidence downward. Four stages out of seven is four stages done, not "it isn't finished." Decide the reading while clear-headed, not at 6pm when tired. Same mechanism as picking the alcohol number before the pub.

- Initiation difficulty is uniform across wanted and unwanted activities. This is the key diagnostic: it rules out avoidance or reluctance as the mechanism.
- Unstructured time is not producing rest. The full cost of doing nothing is being paid with none of the benefit received. The real trade is structure vs. the current state, not structure vs. rest.

## After the Holiday

This format is intended to outlive the break. Two candidate uses:

1. **Scheduled deload weeks** through to May 2027 — the same anchor mechanism, run deliberately rather than reactively.
2. **Any low-initiation stretch** during normal weeks, where the work routine supplies anchors Monday–Friday but weekends don't.

Keep it to four fields. If logging costs more effort than the task, it becomes another thing needing initiation — which is what killed the morning mobility sequence.
