# Holiday Deload Plan — Weeks 9–10

**Context**: First scheduled effort-break of the project, applied to its biggest test case. 8-week re-entry plan completes at end of Week 8. Weeks 9–10 are annual leave, including a London trip Thursday of Week 8 through Tuesday evening of Week 9. Return to work and the routine begins Week 11.

**Purpose**: Replace the historical pattern (complete stop, unrestricted diet/alcohol, "square one" by day 14) with a decoupled reset — real rest, without a full structural collapse. This is not about holding the full routine on holiday. It's about holding a floor.

**Governing principle**: Decide the rules now, while motivated and clear-headed, not in the moment. Every rule below exists so a decision doesn't have to be made spontaneously while on the trip.

---

## Week 8 (final week of the 8-week plan)

Full routine — 4/4 target, as normal. Monday and Thursday runs, Tuesday Pilates + BodyPump. Depart for London Thursday evening/Friday, after the Thursday run.

No new changes this week. Consolidate, don't experiment.

---

## Week 9, Part A — London (Thu departure – Tue evening return)

**No structured sessions planned or expected here.** This is deliberate — don't try to protect BodyPump, Pilates, or runs during the trip itself. The goal is a floor, not a routine.

### The floor

| Element | Plan |
|---|---|
| Walking | No target needed — a city trip like this will likely produce 15–20k+ steps most days on its own. This counts as real activity, not a replacement for training, but a genuine floor under total inactivity. |
| Alcohol | **Decide the number now, not in the pub**: target 1 pint on a typical day out, with one planned evening allowed to go to 2–3 (pick which evening in advance if possible). This isn't elimination — it's replacing "impulse" with "a number decided on a clear-headed Wednesday." |
| Breakfast | Doesn't need to be porridge, but keep it a real breakfast rather than skipped-then-fast-food. A café breakfast counts. The point is not "perfect," it's "not zero structure." |
| Everything else (lunches, dinners, snacks) | Fully off — this is a holiday, eat what the trip offers. No tracking, no guilt. |

### One thing to decide before you go
Pick the alcohol "bigger" evening in advance if you can — e.g. "Saturday night out" gets the 2–3 pint allowance, every other day is the 1-pint default. Having already decided removes the in-the-moment negotiation.

---

## Week 9, Part B — Home, unstructured (Wed–Sun after return)

This is the part of the break that matters most for the reset — it's unstructured time *at home*, which is exactly where the old full-stop pattern used to fully take hold. This is where "a floor" earns its keep.

| Day | Target |
|---|---|
| Wed | Rest / recovery from travel. No exercise expected. Breakfast stays intact if at all possible — this is the easiest day to let it slide by default, so treat it as day one of holding it. |
| Thu | Pilates (30 min) — protect this. Highest value, lowest barrier: no gym trip, no soreness cost, and it's the session most likely to keep the thoracic/core consolidation from fully resetting. |
| Fri | Free / rest |
| Sat | One run — short and easy is fine, 4–5km. Not about distance, about keeping "running" as a thing that still happens rather than a thing that stopped. |
| Sun | Free / golf if it happens |

**Alcohol for this stretch**: no fixed daily cap, but same principle as London — decide roughly what a "normal day" and a "bigger day" look like before you're in one, rather than defaulting to holiday-mode by inertia.

**Diet**: breakfast stays roughly intact (porridge or equivalent). Everything else can be relaxed — this is the explicit decoupling from the plan's own deload principle: dietary relaxation doesn't need to be tied to training collapse.

---

## Week 10 — Home, full holiday week

By this point you're rested from travel and this is the stretch where the "square one" pattern has historically done the most damage, because there's no trip novelty to distract from it, just unstructured time and old defaults.

**Minimum viable week** (adapted from the plan's existing minimum-viable-week concept):

| Element | Target |
|---|---|
| Pilates | 2 sessions this week if possible (e.g. Tue + Fri) — this is the one that most needs frequency, not just eventual return, per pilates-baseline.md |
| Running | 2 easy runs, any distance that feels good — not about hitting 15k/week, about not letting the pattern go fully cold |
| BodyPump | Optional — skip without concern if it doesn't fit; the soreness cost on return to Week 11 is expected either way, and it's a lower priority to protect during a break than Pilates |
| Breakfast | Porridge, most days — doesn't need to be 7/7, just not abandoned |
| Alcohol | Normal holiday latitude, same "decide roughly what bigger days look like" principle as above — no hard number needed here since there's no trip-specific risk, just don't let it become daily by default |
| Shopping | Place at least one Click & Collect order mid-break — keeps the habit alive so Week 11 re-entry isn't starting completely cold |

---

## Week 11 — Return to work and full routine

Full routine resumes: Monday 8k, Tuesday Pilates + BodyPump, Thursday 7k. Expect some BodyPump soreness on return regardless of what happened in Week 10 — that's normal re-entry cost, not a sign anything went wrong.

**Before Week 11 starts**: place a Click & Collect order that covers the first few days back, so the very first week isn't also fighting an empty fridge — same logic as the original plan's Week 8 note about setting up faster re-entry after a break.

---

## What Success Looks Like Here

Not: a perfect two weeks. Not: matching the discipline of Weeks 1–8.

Success is specifically: **genuine rest, an intact breakfast habit, at least a few Pilates sessions and a couple of runs across the two weeks, alcohol that was elevated but bounded rather than unrestricted, and a Week 11 that starts from a partial baseline instead of zero.**

The test this break is actually running is whether decoupling "rest" from "total collapse" is possible for you — the answer to that matters more for the rest of the year than any single number in this document.
