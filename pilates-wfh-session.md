# Pilates — Mid-Week Sessions

**Last updated**: August 2026 (Week 8 of 8-week re-entry plan)
**Status**: Finalised design — **not yet started**. Planned first exposure during the Weeks 9–10 break, at deliberately low effort, to learn the sequences. Full start: Week 11.

**This file supersedes** all previous WFH Pilates specifications, including those in `pilates-baseline.md` and `golf-swing-practice.md`. Where those files conflict with this one, this file is correct.

---

## The Weekly Structure

| Session | When | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** | Tuesday 7:30am | Full 30-minute Downdog session (see `pilates-baseline.md`) | 30 min |
| **Session A — Mobility** | Tuesday, immediately post-BodyPump | Thoracic rotation range | ~14 min |
| **Session B — Stability** | Thursday, immediately post-run | Control of that range — anti-rotation | ~13 min |

### Why this split exists

Sessions A and B are doing **opposite jobs on the same axis**, and neither substitutes for the other.

Session A increases available thoracic rotation. Session B builds the capacity to hold the pelvis and ribcage still while that rotation is used. Mobility without control is not neutral — new range the trunk can't stabilise gets filled by the lumbar spine, which is the exact compensation pattern driving the post-golf soreness in the first place.

This is also why Session B opens with Thread the Needle rather than going straight into stability work: mobilise the range, then train the control of it. Anti-rotation work performed on a stiff thoracic spine reinforces the compensation rather than correcting it.

### Why these specific timings

Both sessions are attached to an existing behaviour rather than initiated independently. The activation cost is already paid — changed, warmed up, in the room, session finished. This is the same structural mechanism as the porridge swap and the Click & Collect order, and it is the only mechanism that has reliably worked.

Attaching Session B to the run rather than to Thursday means it moves when the run moves. If the run slides to Friday, so does the session. That is intended.

**Thursday sequencing note**: the order is run → session → anything else. Thursday is an office day and historically a beer evening; the 13 minutes go before that, not after. Decide this once, not weekly.

## Session A — Mobility (Tuesday, post-BodyPump, ~14 min)

Done immediately after BodyPump ends, before leaving the gym. Body is already warm — no separate warm-up needed. The supine spinal twist serves as the transition out of the class.

| # | Exercise | Time | Notes |
|---|----------|------|-------|
| 1 | Supine Spinal Twist | 90 sec | Knees to chest, drop to one side, head and shoulders rotate opposite — both sides. This is the version the BodyPump instructor uses. |
| 2 | Thread the Needle (both sides) | 3 min | **Priority exercise** — 4–5 slow reps per side, hips stay square, pause at end range |
| 3 | Butterfly Pose | 90 sec | Soles together, knees falling out — let gravity work, don't press the knees down |
| 4 | Supine Windshield Wiper | 2 min | Knees-bent version. Butterfly immediately before makes this noticeably more accessible. |
| 5 | Child's Pose with Lateral Reach (both sides) | 2 min | 30 sec per side, repeated twice each side |
| 6 | Supine Pelvic Lifts | 90 sec | Activation, not stretch — feel it in the glutes |
| 7 | Easy Seat with Thoracic Rotation | 2 min | Pelvis still, slow rotation both sides, pause at end range |

**Total: ~14 minutes**

**Before leaving**: decide where you're doing this *before* BodyPump ends — gym stretching area, studio floor, or a quiet corner. The decision needs to be settled in advance, not made spontaneously when tired after class.

## Session B — Stability (Thursday, post-run, ~13 min)

Done immediately after the Thursday run. Body is warm from the run — no warm-up needed. Thread the Needle at the front is the mobility input; everything after it is control work.

| # | Exercise | Time | Notes |
|---|----------|------|-------|
| 1 | Thread the Needle (both sides) | 2.5 min | Shorter dose than Session A — 3–4 slow reps per side. Purpose here is to open the range before loading it. |
| 2 | Dead Bug | 2.5 min | **Priority exercise.** Lower back stays pressed to the floor throughout — this is the whole point of the movement |
| 3 | Bird Dog | 2.5 min | Slow, with a 2-second pause at full extension. Hips level — no rotation |
| 4 | Side Plank (both sides) | 2 min | 20–30 sec holds, 2 per side. Shorten holds if the run has left the glutes tired — quality over duration |
| 5 | Pallof Press (both sides) | 2.5 min | **The direct anti-rotation exercise.** Requires a resistance band — see equipment note below |
| 6 | Child's Pose | 60 sec | Decompression finish |

**Total: ~13 minutes**

### Equipment note

Pallof Press needs a resistance band anchored at roughly chest height — a door anchor or a band looped around a bannister or door handle works. This is the one item in either session requiring kit. A basic long resistance band is inexpensive and worth having before Week 11.

**If no band is available**, substitute **Plank Shoulder Taps** — from a high plank, tap the opposite shoulder with each hand in turn, keeping the hips completely still. It's a genuine bodyweight anti-rotation exercise and hits the same pattern, though less directly than the Pallof. The tell in both cases is identical: if the hips rock, the exercise isn't working.

## Key Form Notes

**Thread the Needle** — the most important mobility exercise in the week and the last thing to shorten if time is tight.
- On all fours (tabletop), one arm threads under the body
- Rotation happens through the **mid-back** (thoracic spine), not the lower back
- **Hips stay square to the floor throughout** — the most common failure point. If the hips rotate, the movement shifts into the lumbar spine and defeats the purpose
- Go slower than feels necessary — this is a controlled rotation drill, not a stretch to reach further
- 4–5 reps per side in Session A, 3–4 in Session B, pausing briefly at end range

**Dead Bug** — the most important stability exercise, and the easiest to do wrong in a way that feels fine.
- On your back, arms up, knees at 90°. Lower the opposite arm and leg slowly, then return
- **The lower back must stay in contact with the floor for the entire movement.** If it arches, you've gone too far — reduce the range
- Slow. If it feels easy, you're moving too fast or losing the back position
- Breathe out as the limbs lower; holding the breath makes the bracing artificial

**Bird Dog** — already familiar from the Tuesday session, but the emphasis here is different.
- The goal is not reaching further, it's the hips not moving. Imagine balancing a glass of water on your lower back
- 2-second pause at full extension, then return under control
- If the hips tilt or rotate as the leg lifts, shorten the leg extension

**Side Plank**
- Elbow under shoulder, body in a straight line, hips lifted
- Don't let the top hip roll forward or back — the shoulders and hips stay stacked
- Knees-bent version is a legitimate regression if 20 seconds isn't holding cleanly

**Pallof Press**
- Stand side-on to the anchor, band at chest height, hands together at the sternum
- Press the hands straight out and back in. The band is trying to rotate you; your job is to not let it
- **Nothing else moves.** The resistance is felt as a rotational pull that you resist, not a pressing exercise for the arms
- Feet planted, hips square to the front throughout

**Butterfly Pose** — placed immediately before Supine Windshield Wiper in Session A.
- Let gravity do the work — don't press the knees down
- Addresses a real restriction (adductor and inner thigh tightness) limiting Windshield Wiper range. Expect slow improvement over 8–12 weeks

**Easy Seat Thoracic Rotation** — the application layer at the end of Session A.
- Reinforces the Thread the Needle pattern upright, closer to how rotation is used in the golf swing
- Pelvis stays still; only the upper body rotates
- Don't skip it if short on time — it's doing different work from Thread the Needle despite the same target area

## Time Management

**Session A** — if it runs short, trim Supine Pelvic Lifts (to 60 sec) or Child's Pose lateral reach (to 90 sec). Thread the Needle and Easy Seat Rotation are the last to shorten. Do not trim Butterfly Pose — it is load-bearing for the exercise immediately after it.

**Session B** — if it runs short, trim the Child's Pose finish, then Side Plank. Thread the Needle and Dead Bug are the last to shorten. Pallof Press is the exercise with the least redundancy elsewhere in the week, so protect it ahead of Bird Dog if a choice is needed.

---

## Timing

Use an interval timer app (e.g. **Seconds** on iOS/Android) with named intervals at the durations above. Set up Session A and Session B as two saved programmes during the Weeks 9–10 break. Run without thinking.

Downdog is not suitable for either session — it cannot build custom sequences.

---

## Starting Protocol (Weeks 9–10 break)

**Do one of each, at deliberately low effort, purely to learn the sequences.** The deload plan already has Pilates slots available (Thursday of Week 9, two sessions in Week 10) — use those rather than adding volume.

Objectives for these two exposures:
1. Learn what each exercise actually is, particularly Dead Bug and Pallof Press
2. Find out whether the durations are right in practice
3. Build the two Seconds programmes
4. Confirm the band anchor point at home

This is a rehearsal, not a training block. Do not attempt to make them hard.

**The real risk with these sessions is not physical fatigue** — 27 minutes of low-load mobility and stability work is trivial next to 15k of running plus BodyPump. The risk is decision load: two more things per week needing to be initiated, timed and remembered. That is what killed the morning mobility sequence. Both anchor points and both Seconds programmes exist specifically to reduce that to near zero.

## Why This Design Works

The Tuesday anchor session establishes thoracic rotation patterns. Without mid-week frequency, the nervous system partially resets the pattern over six days, meaning each Tuesday is partly re-establishment rather than consolidation. That is the mechanism this file exists to fix.

Note the spacing carefully: because Session A sits on Tuesday evening, both Tuesday touchpoints are effectively one dose. **Session B on Thursday is what actually creates the frequency** — it is the only thing preventing a seven-day gap in rotation work. This is why Thread the Needle appears at the front of Session B, and why Session B cannot be moved to Wednesday. Wednesday is too close to Tuesday to do the job.

The resulting pattern: rotation work on Tuesday and Thursday, longest gap of four days, which is inside the window where patterns reinforce rather than fade. Stability work sits on top of it once a week, which is enough for a low-load pattern to consolidate.

See `pilates-baseline.md` for the Tuesday 30-minute session and `golf-swing-practice.md` for the pre-swing primer and the wider lower-back protocol.
